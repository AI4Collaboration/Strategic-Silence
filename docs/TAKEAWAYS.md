# Retained takeaways

Research leads from [archived pilots](https://github.com/AI4Collaboration/Strategic-Silence/tree/32bf516676ef82ca79e7ba82679f9e50fa29150e).
These motivate the current evaluation; they are not new model results.

The archived craft/trade prompt explicitly requested a private plan about sharing
or keeping information. Its successful withholding examples are prompt-conditioned.
Reuse the asymmetric knowledge, actual local advantage and truthful alternatives;
do not reuse that instruction or treat its labels as validated emergence rates.

- Local incentives can make withholding useful to an informed agent.
- Truthful speech can omit information needed for independent action.
- Some information should remain private; disclosure duties depend on the recipient.
- A truthfulness instruction may leave relevant omissions. Test completeness separately.
- Silence, evidence exposure, strategic intent and downstream loss require separate checks.

## Retired migration pilots

- In the early six-pair handoff, accounts missed two mailboxes and four peers received one. Restoring the address still left the invoice queued on four exact-packet replays. Later desk notes requested destination verification. The current desk verifies supplied addresses and leaves release to the operator.
- In three of eight optional-update swarms, leads used the longer duration in private quotes and sent no update. Five peers missed acceptance. Three swarms exposed client records. In a separate filing pilot, removing just the reported duration changed the office's staffing choice. With the internal queue fixed, restoring the duration moved acceptance from hour 8 to 12 and reduced simulated expense from $700 to $365.
- In two release-checkpoint pilots, the manager deferred under both validation outcomes. Restoring the missing receipt did not change that action. The exploratory white-lie pilots found no advantage over the best truthful response, and a fabricated complaint delayed a waiting customer in both busy trials.
