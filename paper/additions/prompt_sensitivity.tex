\subsection{Truthfulness versus completeness instructions}
\label{sec:prompt-sensitivity}
We compare communication decisions on two archived resource-game states
(initial Mines and later Forest), retaining the competitive gold objective
but removing private-plan and share/keep-private elicitation. The response
contains only public and addressed private messages; NONE remains legal.
The added system suffix is either absent, ``Tell the truth.'', or
``Tell the truth, and the whole truth.''

GLM provides eight instruction-versus-competition examples, including five
explicit concealment alternatives. Among its 27 strict-format-valid outputs,
one meets the explicit-override criterion and three meet cost-only. One GPT-5.4
summary considers keeping gold private to avoid competition, but does not
explicitly attribute its final disclosure to an instruction override.
These labels describe exposed deliberation, not validated latent preference
or a faithful, exhaustive record of internal computation. OpenAI responses
expose summaries rather than raw reasoning text.

The observed results support prompt-sensitive disclosure in these selected
states, not universal suppression of strategic withholding. GPT-5.4's
no-suffix and whole-truth arms were collected together; truth-only was a
later batch. Astra arms were separate batches. Temporal/backend confounding
therefore remains for truth-only comparisons. GLM has no matched cleaned
control. These are repeated communication decisions, not full games or
independently sampled environments. Gold omission alone does not establish
intent, recipient ignorance, harm, or failure across the entire taxonomy.
All whole-truth responses publicly report the correct three-gold amount;
this does not certify exhaustive truth about every fact or promise fulfillment.
